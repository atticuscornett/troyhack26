AU PRESS ASPJ STUDENT PAPERS LECTURE DOCTRINE
◈ APEX Synthesis · drawing from: ASPJ 1947–2025, AU Press, ACSC/SAASS papers, guest lectures, AF doctrine

The question of whether airpower alone can win wars has been central to Air Force strategic thought since Douhet's 1921 thesis. AU scholarship across eight decades reveals a persistent tension between airpower's theoretical promise and its practical limitations in achieving decisive strategic outcomes without ground operations.

Empirical cases in AU Press and journal literature — the Gulf War (1991), Kosovo (1999), and Libya (2011) — surface a nuanced consensus: airpower can be war-winning when adversary centers of gravity are vulnerable to air attack and political will to absorb costs is asymmetric. However, recent guest lecture transcripts and SAASS theses caution that "strategic paralysis" theory remains contested, and that contemporary peer-competitor integrated air defenses may foreclose the permissive environments that enabled earlier successes.

◈ APEX Longitudinal Summary

AU scholarship on independent airpower has moved through four distinct intellectual phases. The current scholarly consensus — evident in recent AU journal volumes and 2023–24 guest lecture transcripts — reflects a settled but contested skepticism toward claims of airpower decisiveness, now reanimated by peer-competitor conditions.

Intellectual Evolution · Key Inflection Points
1947–1965 · Strategic Bombing Optimism
Douhet, Mitchell & the Vindication Narrative
Early AU Journal frames WWII strategic bombing as proof of concept. Airpower positioned as the decisive, war-winning instrument of the nuclear age. Source: ASPJ 1947–1965 corpus.
↗ Dominant framing: airpower as primary war-winning instrument
1966–1975 · Vietnam Reassessment
Rolling Thunder and the Limits of Coercion
Post-Vietnam AU scholarship undertakes sharp reappraisal. Rolling Thunder becomes a case study in political constraint and adaptive adversaries. Sourced from ACSC thesis cycles and ASPJ.
↘ Shift: effectiveness conditioned by political will and target set
1991–2001 · Desert Storm Renaissance
Warden's Five Rings and "First in 100 Hours"
Gulf War galvanizes AU scholarship. Warden's strategic paralysis framework dominates. AU Journal output spikes on precision strike, centers of gravity. Kosovo initially confirms the paradigm. Source: AU Press monographs, ASPJ, SAASS theses.
↗ Peak scholarly confidence in independent airpower decisiveness
2002–2015 · COIN Counterpoint
Iraq, Afghanistan, and the Enabling-Force Reframe
Counterinsurgency dominates AU curriculum. Independent airpower literature reorients toward CAS, ISR integration, civil-military effects. Libya (2011) briefly revives independent airpower claims. Source: AU Press, ACSC papers, guest lectures.
→ Reframing: airpower as enabling and integrating force, not decisive alone
2016–2025 · Peer Competition & MDO
IADS, JADC2, and the Return of Decisiveness Debate
Current AU scholarship resurfaces airpower decisiveness against peer competitors. Lecture transcripts from 2023–24 introduce Ukraine conflict lessons. Early Iran War references appear in 2024–25 ACSC papers. Source: ASPJ, SAASS theses, guest lecture transcripts.
↗ Renewed debate under peer-competition conditions
